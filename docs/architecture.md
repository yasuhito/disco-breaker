# Tutorial architecture

The vertical slice intentionally has three small interfaces.

`TutorialState` owns the deterministic seven-stage state machine. UI input becomes one of five commands (`tap`, `inspect`, `next`, `skip`, `restart`), making the same flow usable from touch, headless scripts, and tests. `semantic_state()` (schema `disco-breaker-tutorial-state.v2`) is the stable machine-readable boundary; the Web view publishes it as `window.discoBreakerState`. Besides the board, floors and inspection it states the flow explicitly: `inspection_mode` (`auto` or `call`), `inspected`, `passed`, `can_call_foreman`, `can_continue`, `fault_revealed`, `leak_seen`, `skipped`, `progress` and the foreman's single latest `utterance` (`kind` start, mistake, ready, unsafe, safe or done, and its text).

`InspectionSemantics.inspect()` receives residual red and blue supports plus one boundary cut for each family. Each class is computed independently as intersection parity modulo two. Only after that classification does it select a witness for visual explanation. The 3 × 3 trap therefore fails because its red support has odd crossing parity, not because a red path happened to be drawable.

`tutorial_view.gd` renders into a fixed 390 × 844 design space and letterboxes uniformly at other aspect ratios; `lesson_artwork.gd` draws the lesson screen from the published state. The board accepts taps from the start: there is no dialogue gate. The view knows presentation and hit coordinates but never decides whether a floor is safe.

## Surface-code rules

The rules follow SyndromeOut's rotated surface code; the implementation here is independent. Connection boxes are data qubits. Red wiring is X correction and flips incident Z-check floors (drawn red); blue wiring is Z correction and flips incident X-check floors (drawn blue). Both wiring contains both components. Each interior check is the parity of the four surrounding residual components, with two-qubit checks at the corresponding boundaries. A move toggles every incident check, including previously dark floors.

The generated face geometry is shared by syndrome computation and floor illumination. Boundary checks appear as half floors. A syndrome-free residual is safe precisely when its X intersection with the top row and its Z intersection with the left column are both even, matching the opposing logical operators. The trap's residual is a full vertical X string, not a diagonal drawing.

Reference: https://github.com/stn/SyndromeOut (rules inspected at a1a3c53cdc07f15e472b204eba478ec0420c1a92).

All seven tutorial floors are now 3 × 3. Final practice uses an X error at the center and a Z error at the upper-right corner, repaired by one red placement and one blue placement (three taps). The earlier 5 × 5 rules regression is retained only as a test fixture; no extra game mode is exposed.

Fault-floor animation is a stateless failing-lamp envelope, keyed independently by stage, color and face. Each lamp has a separate phase/period and deterministic per-cycle short dropout bursts, a weak recovery and long bright intervals. Each floor's under-glass illumination follows the envelope; a faint colored perimeter remains visible during dropouts and disappears when repaired. No lamp object is drawn at the floor center. Reduced-motion mode uses steady light. No animation randomness enters syndrome state or logical inspection.

Boundary lesson (lesson 6): on a 3 × 3 floor, clear the single red edge syndrome by tapping the upper-left box once, then clear the single blue edge syndrome by tapping the upper-right box twice. Interior placements flip two incident checks; these edge placements flip one. Each exercise uses the same syndrome and logical inspection as final practice, accepting stabilizer-equivalent corrections. The blue half starts only through a deliberate Continue after the red pass, so a stale extra tap edits the red floor instead of skipping ahead. The final optional review is the seventh lesson.


## Lesson flow

The seven lessons keep their ids, order and boards; the approved proposal (`disco-pixel-design` at `0768655`, `docs/design/pixel-system-2026-10-31/README.md`, 「7 レッスンの流れ（案）」) changes how they are taught. Lessons 1-3 and 6 inspect automatically whenever the floor becomes dark; lessons 4, 5 and 7 wait for 「親方を呼ぶ」, which is enabled only on a dark floor that has not yet been inspected. A lesson passes, and the bottom button becomes 「つづける」, only when the floor is dark and the inspection of exactly this board is safe. Any tap clears the verdict, so editing after a pass disables Continue again, and a safe reading of a lit board (the function still returns one) never enables completion.

The game calls a socket where the original fault and the player's wiring differ a leftover (「直し残し」, `_residual_support`, fault XOR wiring per colour). A floor's lamp sees only its own sockets: an odd count of leftovers lights it, an even count leaves it dark. A dark floor leaks when the leftovers cross the foreman's inspection line (red: top row, blue: left column) an odd number of times; the verdict is still `InspectionSemantics.inspect()`. The electrical wording is a metaphor; no electrical rules exist.

Lesson 5 is observe-then-repair. The untouched trap (red at top and bottom, fault at the centre) is dark and leaking; calling the foreman shows the leak and the explanation 「漏電あり！床は2本で暗い。赤が上から下へ抜けた。上下を外し、破線に赤を。」, which stays until the board changes, and does not pass. The dashed original fault then stays visible for the repair. Any dark and safe board passes the next inspection: the seven-tap exact repair, the three-tap minimal ones (red added at the left column) and loop-shaped leftovers alike. A player who repairs before ever calling the foreman is not trapped: the first inspection of the repaired board passes; the hint for a lit board then asks to darken it first (no dashed fault was shown yet).

The foreman's bubble holds one utterance, chosen from the current board: the lesson's instruction until the first change, then a hint for a lit floor (specific hints such as 「赤になった。もう1回で青になる。」 only while they describe the board), a prompt to call the foreman on a dark callable floor, a leak line naming the colours the inspection found (「暗いけど漏電！」 in automatic lessons, 「漏電あり！」 when called, 「まだ漏電。」 for a repeated leak in lesson 5) or the lesson's praise. Leak lines are red, praise is bold green and hints are brown, over a paper bubble with a 45-degree tail. Finishing shows 「全7レッスン修了！」; skipping the practice shows a different line and no pass.

The leak presentation follows the proposal's order within about two seconds: leftover rings and each watching floor's 「2」, then the leak route (`LeakFeedback.route`, drawn through socket and floor centres at 45 and 90 degrees) with the inspection line's odd count, then cracks and the dashed original fault with its caption in place of the legend. Input stays available throughout; any change cancels the presentation and its stale verdict. Reduced motion shows the final frame at once. Both colours leaking show both routes, counts and dashed faults.

In lesson 4 a called inspection is presented first: the foreman says 「上の段と左の列を数えるぞ。」 and traces the red inspection line, then the blue one (1.6 seconds), before his verdict, the celebration or the leak presentation appear. The model's verdict is immediate; the view publishes `counting` and `continue_enabled` beside it, and Continue is drawn disabled and ignores taps while counting, so it can never race the presentation. Any edit or home ends the count (with any pending celebration or discharge); reduced motion never starts it.


## Visual direction and input

The lesson screen is a direct port of the proposal's playable mock (`source/art.js`): the same layout in 195 × 422 units of 2 design px (home, a progress bar that grows over the seven lessons, audio plate, lesson title, the foreman and his bubble, the board housing, the wire legend and the bottom button), the same palette and the same flat shapes, all with horizontal, vertical or 45-degree edges, chamfers and ink outlines. Shapes are flat polygons with a sub-device-pixel antialiased rim, as the title's dynamic shapes. Floors use the shared deterministic lamp envelope quantised to lit, dim and dropout, with family-coded stripes; red wiring is always 「\」 and blue always 「/」 on every socket (a display rule only), both crossing as an X. The foreman breathes, blinks, talks for 0.7 seconds after a new utterance and hops with a thumbs-up on a pass; the progress bar grows and the newly enabled button hops once. Reduced motion stops all of it and shows the final state. The optional skip (and, after completion, 「タイトルへ」) is a secondary plate between the legend and the bottom button, which the three-lesson mock does not show. The lesson's published state is drawn; it is not recomputed for every animation frame. Japanese copy uses bundled Noto Sans CJK JP; headings and bold lines use a slightly emboldened FontVariation of the same face. Utterances use explicit line breaks, and tests measure every line against the bubble. Font attribution, OFL licenses and subset reproduction are recorded in `assets/fonts/README.md`.

Every plate is exactly its hit area: each connection box reacts in its whole 92 × 92 px cell, the bottom button in its 356 × 64 px plate, home in 44 × 44 and audio in 80 × 44. A disabled button does nothing.

The view handles native touch and mouse input directly. Mouse emulation from touch is disabled so one physical tap advances exactly one wire state. CI includes a native-touch cycle regression alongside the seven-stage mouse-driven flow. `scripts/check.sh` also parses the view explicitly, because an editor import alone may report a script error without returning a failing process status.


## Reward feedback

`RewardFeedback` observes input-driven state transitions, not frames. Extinguishing the last syndrome is a neutral, silent event; only a newly passed lesson (dark floor, explicitly safe inspection) receives a success sting, sparks around the socket and the foreman's approval. Failed repairs stay editable on the same board; no lesson, including the crossing trap, advances on a leak, so the full tutorial has eight verified rewards (lesson 5 after its repair, both halves of lesson 6). Lesson 1's pass (「いいぞ！ 赤い床のちらつきが直ったね！」) plays its own original synthesized bell (`assets/sounds/lesson1_success_bell.wav`, imported uncompressed; generator and provenance in `assets/sounds/README.md` and `tools/success_sound/`); every other lesson keeps the procedural sting. The final practice uses the longer sting, and skip/replay/duplicate observations never create success.

The original synth stings are generated once as bounded 16-bit PCM (0.44/0.72 seconds); lesson 1's bell is a 0.75-second 48 kHz file made by the same kind of code offline. The speaker button persists mute, stops an active sound immediately, and never replays a missed sound on unmute. Master audio mute is respected. Reduced motion uses the static approval badge and normal result copy without sweeps, particles or portrait motion. Effects do not block input and are cleared when leaving their floor. `window.discoBreakerFeedback` exposes event and playback-start counts for browser verification, separately from game state.


## Electrical failure and lamp audio

`LeakFeedback` observes a newly revealed, explicitly unsafe inspection. It triggers one brief discharge, independently of successful rewards, and resets on any board change, stage/substep changes and replay. `LeakFeedback.route()` is presentation only: after the parity verdict, it traverses the same-color check graph using actual residual qubits to find a boundary-to-boundary chain. Red and blue are rendered independently. The old red preferred-node list is no longer used for drawing; neither a logical cut nor arbitrarily ordered support is presented as a physical chain.

A white pulse travels along that residual route while it is drawn and then keeps circulating; the floors it crosses show fine, static 45/90-degree cracks. These overlays disappear with the verdict at the next change. They cannot change syndromes, corrections or inspection. Only a failed inspection reveals the original error, as dashed wires on the same band as the player's solid wires (in lesson 5 it stays for the repair); matching components get a small 0 marker and are absent from the residual route. The caption says this is a tutorial reveal, not information provided by the floors.

The failure snap and small fluorescent ticks are original bounded procedural PCM. `LampSoundGate` samples the exact independent brightness envelope used by each visible floor and sounds only a bright-to-dropout transition during active play. It permits one voice, at least 0.32 seconds between ticks globally and 1 second per lamp, with three quiet variations. No lamp audio starts before user interaction, after completion, on a dark floor, while muted or in reduced-motion mode. Input transitions stop any current tick and discard old phase history while preserving cooldowns, so rapid input cannot bypass the rate limit. Failure audio uses the existing mute control and cannot restart on redraw/ignored taps. The Web feedback object exposes separate failure/lamp playback counts for tests; none of this alters game-state semantics.


## Title shell

`tutorial_view.gd` owns a small title/tutorial navigation shell above `TutorialState`. Its published state adds `screen`, `tutorial_started` and `campaign_available` without modifying the tutorial model contract. Hidden tutorial inputs are ignored. Home preserves the in-memory model (board, verdict, progress), stops audio, and settles transient effects; Continue never replays old sounds, presentations or the foreman's speech, which animates only for a new or restarted lesson or a new line. Entry and Replay have a brief input guard, counted from the moment the lesson actually opens, because the title's tutorial plate overlaps the lesson's bottom button, so a rapid second tap cannot pass or skip a lesson. Finished or skipped lessons can return to title or restart through Replay. The Web build also writes the foreman's latest line and the button state to a visually hidden polite live region.

`title_artwork.gd` draws the title from the approved title mock (its 195 × 422 layout units are 2 design px): flat background bands, the mirror ball hanging on its cord above the two-line DISCO BREAKER sign, a diamond glass floor, the disabled 「スタート」 (「準備中」) plate, the 「チュートリアル」 plate without a chevron, and an audio plate. Every edge outside text glyphs is horizontal, vertical or diagonal, with flat colours, chamfers and ink outlines. The sign is built from full and half (45-degree) letter cells dilated by a regular octagon; it is written as one SVG and rasterised by Godot at the device scale (only when the scale changes), so overlapping cells form one antialiased union with holes. Other static surfaces are one vertex-coloured mesh with a one-device-pixel alpha fringe. Floor glass is plain; lit lamps differ only by colour and use the shared deterministic lamp envelope quantised to lit, dim and dropout. The title's audio plate (three angular waves for ON, one 45-degree slash for OFF, icon and measured label centred together) shares the mute state. Each title plate's hit area is exactly its drawn plate (the tutorial plate's lip included). No title audio autoplays.

An accepted tap on the tutorial plate (a fresh entry, a resume or a replay after completion) plays `assets/sounds/title_tutorial_press.wav` once, on its own player so that nothing stopped on entry can cut the 90 ms sound; only mute stops it, and a muted press is silent. The plate then dips in and comes back over `PRESS_TIME` (0.15 s; cap, highlight and label at most 4 px down over a shrinking lip, inside the unchanged plate) and the lesson opens when it is back at rest. Further title taps are ignored until then, so rapid mouse or touch input is one press. Reduced motion opens the lesson at once. At rest the plate draws exactly as before; no other button plays the press. `window.discoBreakerFeedback` counts accepted presses and press sound starts separately from rewards.

The mirror ball has no frame: mirrors on rotating latitude rows compress toward the limb and are clipped to the octagon, with seams that thin faster than the mirrors, so only narrow seams meet the silhouette. Rays come from selected mirrors in the top, upper, middle, lower and bottom rows: up-diagonal from the top, sideways from the sides, straight down from the underside. Each leaves its own visible mirror outward on its own side, crosses at most a narrow rim, and fades in and out as the mirror turns (headings change only at zero strength). A free ray runs clear of the sign, audio plate, cord and floor to a scene edge, fading with distance; a straight-down ray ends behind the sign's solid top bar, which is drawn in front of the rays. In this layout every ball-to-floor path would cross the two sign lines, so no ray lands on the floor. Each ray is one quad textured with a filtered cross-section (one bright core, a flat feather and one-device-pixel ramps). Reduced motion holds one phase that shows up, sideways and down rays. `tests/title_geometry.gd` audits edge angles, plain floor tiles, the mirror-only silhouette, ray origins on facets, outward headings, clean paths and endpoints, continuity and the visible spread of rays through a full revolution, lamp flicker, the audio plate padding and reduced-motion stillness.
